Dr Jessica Clark

Infectious disease epidemiologist

Over half the world’s population is at risk of vector-borne diseases (VBDs), which are transmitted to vertebrate hosts by organisms like mosquitos, snails and ticks. VBDs are often zoonotic (ZVBDs) which adds complications for control because they are often spread by multiple generalist vector species, that feed on and transmit disease to a range of animals, and people. Consequently, animal-targeted interventions may offer significant opportunities for zoonotic disease prevention at the source, simultaneously tackling the burden of disease in animals and people. A major challenge in developing interventions in this One Health framework, is the need for detailed epidemiological evidence spanning animal, vector, human, and environmental systems.

In my research, I aim to bridge these diverse disciplines to inform evidence-based disease solutions. I develop mathematical and statistical modelling tools applied to a range of vector-borne and neglected diseases, lead a field data generating platform in Tanzania, and engage with a range of stakeholders to support the design of control and elimination programmes.

I'm currently based in the School of Biodiversity, One Health & Veterinary Medicine at the University of Glasgow.

Research

What I work on

Rift Valley fever virus

I am the Glasgow-based co-Principal Investigor on the CEPI funded REMIT consortium. This consortium brings together an experienced team of mathematical modellers, One Health epidemiologists, clinical researchers, vaccine trial specialists and social scientists to answer the overarching question: Is a human RVF vaccine efficacy trial feasible, and if so, with what design and in what location?

DRIVERS

Schistosomiasis remains a devasting disease in many parts of Africa. Despite extensive efforts, the factors driving outcomes to treatment against the disease remain poorly understood. Drivers brings together researchers working on basic science, field epidemiology and modelling, to work alongside Schistosomiasis Control Programme Managers within the Ministries of Health. I am a Co-I on the DRIVERS team, and together we investigate the causes of treatment failure at both the individual level and community level across diverse eco-epidemiological settings to improve treatment strategies.
