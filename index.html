About me

I'm an infectious disease epidemiologist and mathematical modeller. I build mathematical and statistical models of neglected tropical and vector-borne diseases, with a focus on Rift Valley fever and schistosomiasis.

My work links transmission dynamics, diagnostics and field data to support vaccine development and the design of control and elimination programmes.

I'm based in the School of Biodiversity, One Health & Veterinary Medicine at the University of Glasgow, and I'm the Glasgow Principal Investigator on a CEPI-funded consortium supporting Rift Valley fever vaccine development.

Research overview

Vaccines & seroepidemiology

Rift Valley fever

Dynamic and statistical models of Rift Valley fever virus in livestock and people, including force-of-infection estimation from serological data, to inform where and how new vaccines are trialled and deployed.

Treatment, diagnostics & WASH

Schistosomiasis elimination

Understanding treatment efficacy and treatment failure, reconciling antigen- and egg-based diagnostics, and designing mass drug administration and WASH strategies that move programmes towards elimination.

Bayesian & spatial modelling

Methods for policy

Age-structured transmission models, spatially explicit dynamics and Bayesian causal inference, turned into practical tools that programme managers and policymakers can use.
